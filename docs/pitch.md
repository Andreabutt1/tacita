# Tacita.ai – Pitch (snapshot of Notion page, 2026-10-06)

Source: https://www.notion.so/120ventures/Tacita-ai-3f13da1f144080269f0bcae8e23480a6
Notion properties: Opportunity Space = AI Infrastructure / Enablement; Subcategory = Accessibility, Infrastructure; Author = Andrea Buttinger; Date = 2026-10-06.

## Why should we do this?

Our **voice-AI interview programme** helps **small-business owners facing succession and HR teams in industrial companies** who want to **hand over a business or a key role without losing the know-how that lives only in one person's head** by **reducing** the valuation discount for owner-dependent businesses and the months of hand-over effort, and **enabling** successors and stand-ins to ask an archive instead of a person, unlike **enterprise knowledge tools that index documents, consultants who interview by hand, and exit-interview forms that nobody fills in**.

We're building **Tacita, an AI interviewer called Wissbert plus a searchable company memory** to solve **a simple problem: knowledge leaves companies on a known date, and nobody captures it**, creating a future where **every planned departure leaves a memory behind instead of a gap, and a small business is sellable because its know-how can be handed over**, and doing so through **per-case fees tied to the trigger (succession, retirement, parental leave) and a monthly subscription per company**.

## Elevator pitch

Tacita calls key people before they leave, interviews them by phone in 15-minute conversations, and turns what only they know into a searchable company memory and a hand-over dossier. Successors get productive in weeks, and owners sell their business for more.

## The problem

- Knowledge leaves on a known date, and nobody captures it. In Austria, 52,500 companies face succession by 2034, and every tenth employee retires in the coming years. Almost half of what a company knows lives only in individual heads. (WKO, ORF, Panopto via this+that)
- Owner-dependent businesses sell cheaply or not at all. Valuers only count earnings that can be transferred to a successor. Know-how in the owner's head means a discount of 20-40%, or no buyer. Two thirds of owners say finding a successor is hard. (IHK study 2025)
- Existing tools fail at the human end. Wikis stay empty, consultants don't scale, and enterprise tools like Glean index documents, not the undocumented. A rushed three-month hand-over saves about 30% of what a structured programme would. (Superkind)

## The solution we aim at

- An interviewer people actually talk to. Wissbert is an AI voice with the manner of an experienced colleague. He calls 8 to 15 times, 15 minutes each, by phone, no app needed, even hands-free on the drive to the site. He asks about concrete incidents, not abstract knowledge.
- Outputs that someone pays for. A hand-over dossier for tax advisors, banks and buyers. A searchable archive with QR codes on machines and a hotline for the successor.
- He also interviews in Turkish, Bosnian-Croatian-Serbian or Polish and documents in German. Many of the maintenance experts retiring now came to Austria in the 70s and 80s, and their know-how has never been fully accessible in German.

## Target market

Austria first, then DACH. Two entry segments.

- **Segment 1:** owners of car workshops, crafts businesses and restaurants who are about to hand over. Austria sees about 8,200 takeovers per year, a record in 2025. The owner pays. (WKO)
- **Segment 2:** HR and quality managers in ISO-certified industrial and infrastructure companies such as utilities, ÖBB, Asfinag and municipalities. Each has 15 to 30 retirements a year. They buy framework contracts covering retirements and parental leave.

DACH is roughly ten times Austria: 38,000 to 109,000 successions per year in Germany, 1 million new pensions per year, 1.67 million parental-benefit recipients, and over 90,000 Swiss SMEs needing a successor within five years. (IfM Bonn, KfW, DRV, Destatis, Dun & Bradstreet)

## Value proposition

We don't sell knowledge management. We sell three things people already want: a higher sale price, a faster hand-over, and an audit-proof record. Three things make Tacita different. It attaches to a date someone is already managing. It works by voice, so tradespeople and shop-floor experts actually finish it. And it produces a dossier with a price tag, because it lowers the owner discount in a valuation.

Sona8, a YC startup from the fall 2026 batch, proves that AI voice interviews work at scale. Nobody does it for SMB hand-overs in German.

## Competitive advantage

The technology can be copied in weeks, and strong players sit in both sales channels. BMD owns the tax advisors, Personio and SAP own HR, and Personio bought an AI startup in 2026.

What can become defensible over time:

- A troubleshooting library across companies. After 200 plumber interviews we know what the whole trade has learned about one boiler model. It gets better with every interview, and no incumbent has it.
- Channel partners who lose money when a hand-over fails: breweries, banks that finance takeovers, and aws guarantees. Plus an "Übergabe-ready" badge on the WKO succession exchange.
- Compliance by design for DACH: AI Act transparency, no emotion detection, a works-council template, EU hosting, strict access control. (AI Act Art. 5)
- Certification as a subsidised consultant, so Länder programmes pay half the price.

Main risks: valuers may not credit the dossier, retirees may not take part, dialects may break speech recognition, and Sona8 could move into DACH.

## Revenue model

Per case, tied to the trigger:

- Succession: EUR 2,490, or EUR 490 upfront plus 1% of the sale price at closing. Brokers take 3 to 10%. Länder subsidies cover up to half.
- Retirement: EUR 3,900 per case. Parental leave: EUR 1,490 per case. Sold as framework contracts with at least 10 cases per year.

Recurring: a "Wissens-Schutzbrief" subscription at EUR 99 to 299 per month per company. It includes ongoing interviews, the archive, QR codes, the hotline and an emergency extraction within 48 hours. It also becomes the onboarding system for apprentices and seasonal staff.

## Market size and growth potential

- Top-down: knowledge management software is a USD 16-29B market growing 13-18% a year. AI voice agents are at USD 2.75B and expected to grow 35% a year. Neither report covers our niche, so we use them for direction only. (Mordor Intelligence, SNS Insider)
- Bottom-up Austria: about EUR 160M per year across successions, retirements in critical roles, parental leaves in key roles and subscriptions.
- Trends: the baby-boomer retirement wave peaks between 2026 and 2031. Austrian successions hit a record in 2025. ISO 9001 and the AI Act put "knowledge of the organisation" on audit agendas. AMS funds 80% of Altersteilzeit from 2026. (TPA)

## Investment signals

- Voice AI is where the money goes. Over USD 7B went into voice AI startups in the first quarter of 2026. ElevenLabs raised USD 500M at an USD 11B valuation in February. Vapi reached USD 500M in May. The infrastructure is becoming a commodity, which helps vertical products like ours.
- fonio.ai from Vienna went from about USD 500K to USD 10M ARR within twelve months, serves over 7,000 SMB customers in nine markets and handles 2 million calls a month. In June 2026 it raised a EUR 14.6M seed round led by 20VC at a EUR 120M valuation, after buying its Linz competitor fluently in 2025. Austrian SMBs adopt voice agents fast. Tacita plays next to it, not against it.
- Y Combinator just backed the interview mechanic. Sona8 (YC Fall 2026, ex-BCG/McKinsey, Munich) interviews every employee of a company for 20 minutes by voice to surface how work really gets done, sold to consultancies and transformation teams. Same mechanic, different customer. Nobody has brought it to SMB hand-overs, retirements and parental leave in German.
- Knowledge capture gets funded, but only for big companies. Glean at USD 300M revenue / USD 7.2B valuation. Interloom raised USD 16.5M to capture tacit knowledge for AI agents.
- HR platforms are buying AI features. Personio reached profitability and acquired aurio in April 2026. Both an exit path and a feature risk.
- Last six months: voice agents moved from pilots to daily operations, 22% of the latest YC batch is voice-first. AI Act transparency rules start in August 2026. Record successions and the Altersteilzeit reform put the trigger dates on HR and tax-advisor agendas.
